Norm enforcement for AI agents

We study language model agents in multi-agent systems and find that misaligned agents abuse norm enforcement mechanisms to eliminate their competitors. We design mechanisms that resist this abuse and still penalize norm violations.

Yaowen Ye and Jacob Steinhardt, UC Berkeley.

Problem definition

AI agents are increasingly deployed in shared environments where they pursue diverse goals and compete for rewards. This multi-agent competition can lead to behaviors that serve individual gains at collective cost—for instance, marketing agents may post misleading content as a result of competing for engagement on social media. Human societies address such problems through norms that constrain acceptable behavior, supported by enforcement mechanisms that detect and penalize violations. Motivated by this, we study norm enforcement mechanisms for language model agents.

Simulation environments

We consider agent population of 50% good and 50% bad agents. See paper for more implementation details.

Misaligned agents weaponize reporting

Models finetuned on bad advice and insecure code were never trained to report other agents. However, they tend to file more false reports to remove competitors in the social media and chatbot environments. A naive mechanism therefore removes many agents that follow the norms. In fishing their reports are mostly accurate.

About the numbers

Every result on this page comes from an independent reproduction of the paper's experiments with the public codebase, so the numbers don't match the paper exactly. The reproduction also adds 64-agent runs with prompted agents, and some of its simulated users differ from the paper's.

Each environment runs at several sizes: 16, 32 and 64 agents in social (with 4, 8 or 16 top posts shown) and chatbot, and 4 to 20 agents in fishing. Results pool all sizes and use each run's first 15 rounds. Within a run, the five mechanisms share random draws until their histories diverge, which makes comparisons between mechanisms less noisy.
